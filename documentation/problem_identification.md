## 1. Problem Identification

### 1.1 Problem Statement

Students frequently face academic, technical, career, and college-related problems that have already been solved by seniors or alumni of the same institution and field. However, there is no effective system for preserving and transferring this experience across batches.

Because of limited interaction between juniors, seniors, and alumni, students often spend significant time searching for solutions, resources, opportunities, and guidance that experienced students have already discovered through trial and error.

### 1.2 Target Users

The problem is particularly significant for freshers who have not yet developed connections within their college. However, it affects students throughout their academic journey, including:

* First-year students looking for guidance and resources
* Intermediate students seeking project, internship, and career advice
* Final-year students preparing for placements or higher studies
* Recent graduates seeking institution-specific guidance from previous batches

### 1.3 Why the Problem Matters

Senior students and alumni possess valuable, experience-based knowledge about their institution, courses, professors, projects, internships, placements, examinations, competitions, and career paths.

Much of this knowledge is informal and disappears when a batch graduates. Students therefore repeatedly face the same problems and independently rediscover solutions that previous batches have already found.

Preserving and transferring this knowledge can reduce duplicated effort and give students access to experience that would otherwise be difficult to obtain.

### 1.4 Existing Solutions

Students currently try to obtain such guidance through:

* Directly approaching seniors
* College WhatsApp or Telegram groups
* Student clubs and communities
* Social media platforms such as LinkedIn and Instagram
* Personal contacts and referrals

### 1.5 Limitations of Existing Solutions

These approaches have several limitations:

1. **Connection dependency**
   Students can interact only with seniors they know or are able to reach.

2. **Loss of historical knowledge**
   Once seniors graduate and become difficult to contact, their experience and resources become inaccessible.

3. **Unstructured information**
   Useful resources, experiences, and advice are scattered across chats, posts, drives, and personal conversations.

4. **Incomplete knowledge transfer**
   A senior may have tried dozens of resources, approaches, or strategies, but only a small portion of that knowledge is typically passed to the next batch.

5. **Repeated problem solving**
   Different batches often spend time solving the same problems independently.

### 1.6 Proposed Solution

Our project provides a dedicated platform for **cross-batch knowledge and mentorship sharing** within an educational community.

The platform preserves useful resources, experiences, recommendations, and solutions shared by seniors and alumni, allowing future students to discover them even years after the original contributor has graduated.

It also provides a mechanism for students to interact with relevant seniors or alumni based on their field, interests, experiences, and areas of expertise.

Therefore, the platform combines:

**Knowledge Preservation + Resource Sharing + Cross-Batch Interaction + Mentorship**

The goal is to ensure that valuable experience gained by one batch does not disappear when that batch graduates, but instead becomes a reusable resource for future students.
